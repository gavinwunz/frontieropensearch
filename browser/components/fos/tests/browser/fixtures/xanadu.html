Project Xanadu

Ted Nelson's design for a hypertext system in which quotation is a live connection to the original rather than a copy of it.

Xanadu's central idea is transclusion. A passage that appears in two documents is not duplicated; it is the same passage, included by reference, and the reader can see every context it appears in. Links are two-way and never break, because the address is to the content rather than to a location that may be reorganised.

The question is not what a computer can do. The question is what a document can be.

Why it matters here

Because the failure it diagnoses is the one a browser still has. A quotation on the web is a copy with a footnote, and the footnote rots. Nothing in a browser records that two pages answered the same question, which is exactly the relation a research session is made of.
