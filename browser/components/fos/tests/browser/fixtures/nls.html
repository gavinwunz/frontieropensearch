1968 · San Francisco

The Mother of All Demos

Douglas Engelbart shows a live system with a mouse, hypertext, screen sharing and collaborative editing, thirty years before any of it was ordinary.

The demonstration ran for ninety minutes and contained most of the interface ideas the next fifty years would ship one at a time. What did not ship was the part Engelbart cared about most: the chord keyset, the shared view, and the idea that a tool should be learned deliberately because the work is worth the training.

The better we get at getting better, the faster we will get better.

The part that was dropped

Engelbart's system assumed a user willing to learn it. The industry chose the opposite, and the result is an interface that is instantly usable and permanently shallow. A browser is the clearest case: everybody can open a tab, and nobody can find the page they read last Tuesday.
